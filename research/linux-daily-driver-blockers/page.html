First-person trials · Challenge videos · Forum reports

One deal-breaker,
plus a pile-up.

People rarely quit Linux because of a single catastrophic failure. Across daily-driver challenges and return stories, the pattern is consistent: one hard blocker they can't work around — an app, a game, an exam, a piece of hardware — plus a slow accumulation of small friction that costs time until they stop.

Ranking is by recurrence across sources, not a survey Covers 2020–2026 accounts, emphasis 2024–25 Sources linked throughout

The quitting mechanism, in one line

XDA's synthesis of beginners puts it plainly: most people don't leave after one disaster. They leave after fingerprint readers, Bluetooth, sleep drain, monitor order and a dozen other small things add up — on top of the one thing that already forced them to keep Windows around.

“Most beginners don't leave after a single catastrophic failure. They leave because of a slow accumulation of small frustrations.” — XDA, Jan 2026 · 5 reasons beginners go back to Windows

1

The four hard blockers

These appear in almost every source and are sufficient on their own to force a dual-boot or a return. Everything else is friction — these are walls.

01Hard blocker

A specific app that isn't there

Above all Adobe Creative Cloud and Microsoft Office 365 desktop, then Fusion 360 and other CAD, Ableton Live / FL Studio / Pro Tools, and employer- or school-mandated tools. No native version, and the substitute doesn't carry the workflow.

  • Cited in nearly every source — XDA, MakeUseOf, How-To Geek
  • Often it's not the app alone, but its files, plugins and collaborators
02Hard blocker

Kernel anti-cheat in multiplayer games

Valorant / League of Legends, Fortnite, Apex Legends, Destiny 2, GTA Online, Call of Duty, Battlefield / EA titles, Escape from Tarkov. The anti-cheat can support Linux — the publisher hasn't enabled it.

  • Apex is a regression: support was withdrawn 31 Oct 2024
  • Destiny 2 bypass attempts are, per Bungie, “met with a game ban”
03Hard blocker

Work or school lock-in you don't choose

Proctoring software that explicitly bans Linux, narrow MDM / Intune support, and employer VPN / endpoint tools. Often the employer simply issues a Windows or Mac laptop and forbids alternatives.

  • Pearson VUE OnVUE: “All Linux/Unix Operating Systems are strictly prohibited”
  • Intune on Linux: only Ubuntu 22.04/24.04 LTS and RHEL 8/9, GNOME, x86_64, corporate-owned only
04Hard blocker

Hardware that doesn't work — or works without its software

Especially on laptops: fingerprint readers, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth audio, sleep drain, hybrid / Nvidia GPUs, and fan / RGB / peripheral apps. Desktops and ThinkPad / Framework-class machines are usually fine; brand-new and hybrid-GPU laptops are where accounts break down.

  • One 2020 Pop!_OS trial found no Wi-Fi driver at all — and fixing it required internet access (UpThrust, 2020 — single anecdote)

Concrete — no workaround accepted

  • Missing app (Adobe, Office, CAD, DAW)
  • Blocked game (anti-cheat)
  • Blocked exam or job access (proctoring, MDM)
  • Hardware function absent (e.g. no Wi-Fi driver)

Friction — works, at a time cost

  • Pile-up: fingerprint, function keys, Bluetooth reconnects, sleep draining overnight, monitors waking in the wrong order — individually ignorable, collectively the reason people leave (XDA, Jan 2026)
  • Time as the price: “Linux is only ‘free’ to an extent — the cost is your time” — XDA, “I regret switching to Linux” (2026)
  • Unpredictability: XDA's 30-day gaming trial (Oct 2025) returned mainly because games that did work crashed randomly — distinct from the anti-cheat titles he was willing to dual-boot for

The pile-up, time-cost and unpredictability themes each appear in at least 4 independent sources (XDA ×3, MakeUseOf, LTT 2021 & 2026, The Verge).

2

Software gaps — the #1 category

Four different mechanisms get conflated as “the app is missing.” They have different fixes — and different reasons people won't accept them.

a · Missing

The app itself has no Linux version

Adobe CC, Office 365 desktop, Fusion 360 / SolidWorks / AutoCAD, Ableton / FL Studio / Pro Tools, Final Cut Pro, CapCut desktop, Descript.

b · Files & collaborators

An alternative exists, the ecosystem doesn't travel

Premiere .prproj files shared with clients, .docx/.xlsx fidelity, fonts, macros and advanced Excel features. If deliverables depend on them, dual-boot is described as “your only option.”

c · Plugins

The library you paid for doesn't transfer

Premiere / After Effects commercial plugins, VST/AU and iLok-licensed music plugins, Photoshop plugins. iLok is not available for Linux at all.

d · Retraining

Alternative exists but lags — or means relearning

Sometimes stated honestly as unwillingness to relearn. DaVinci Resolve is the major exception — native and genuinely pro — but its free Linux version lacks the H.264/H.265 support the Windows/Mac free versions have.

Named most often

Adobe Photoshop / Premiere / After Effects / Lightroom Microsoft Office 365 desktop Fusion 360 · SolidWorks · AutoCAD · CNC software Ableton Live · FL Studio · Cubase · Pro Tools · Logic Pro Final Cut Pro · CapCut desktop · Descript

MakeUseOf (May 2026) notes Adobe CC and Office 365 “are not natively available… Wine… tied to Windows-specific services like DRM, cloud syncing… won't work or will randomly crash.” Autodesk staff on Autodesk's own forum: “Fusion is not supported on Linux and there are no plans to do so… no major CAD players… [have] a current version natively on Linux.” Sources: MakeUseOf · How-To Geek, Apr 2026 · Autodesk Forums · LinuxAudio.dev FAQ

“Only three things hold me back from using Linux for my daily driver: Lack of Office, Ableton Live and Fusion 360… there are good alternatives there, if I'm willing to trash my substantial license investment and know-how, which I'm not yet.”

— Autodesk forum user, noting a Windows VM also fails for Fusion/Ableton because of inadequate GPU passthrough

What is not a gap: software development (often described as better on Linux), web / light office work, 3D (Blender is native and first-class), streaming/recording (OBS native). Browsers, VS Code, Slack/Zoom web and dev tools are praised across sources. XDA (Jun 2025) is candid about the retraining part: “I'm just not willing to learn another application at the moment… if this were the only thing holding me back, I might just bite the bullet.” XDA, Jun 2025 · Creator gaps also in Linux for Content Creators

Evidence note on Office fidelity: LibreOffice / OnlyOffice open .docx/.xlsx, but formatting, fonts, macros and advanced Excel features are the recurring complaint. In the research this is mostly a pattern of repeated claims in forums and articles, not controlled side-by-side testing — treat it as widely reported, not measured.

3

Gaming: most of it works — the biggest titles don't

Even people who quit concede the shift: most single-player Steam libraries run via Proton, sometimes better than on Windows. The block is publisher anti-cheat decisions, not a technical limit.

XDA's Oct 2025 trial: “shocked at just how many games in my library supported Linux… they ran just as well, or even better.” The Verge's year-long user: “Everything in my Steam library runs great with Proton.” Easy Anti-Cheat and BattlEye can support Linux/Proton — the developer must opt in. Other gaming friction people cite

Unpredictability in games that do run

  • War Thunder and Arma Reforger crashing randomly with unhelpful errors in XDA's 30-day Ubuntu trial (Radeon RX 9070 XT; Mesa drivers didn't fix it) — the author's actual deal-breaker (XDA, Oct 2025)
  • Competitive players reporting titles that run (e.g. Counter-Strike 2, native) still “feel” less responsive — single-source claim (Mem's Tech Tips), treat as anecdote
  • Day-one releases and mods / mod managers recur in forums; evidence thinner

Launchers, peripherals & VR

  • Non-Steam launchers (EA App — reported broken / installer not finishing in tracker summaries; Battle.net, Epic, Ubisoft) need workarounds via Lutris / Heroic / non-Steam Proton entries. The Verge user got WoW working only by adding Battle.net as a non-Steam game
  • Meta Quest PC Link app is Windows-only (ALVR + SteamVR is the workaround)
  • Racing wheels, capture cards and vendor RGB/peripheral software have partial community coverage (OpenRazer, Oversteer) rather than vendor tools
4

Hardware & drivers: laptops are the fault line

Pattern across accounts: desktops and ThinkPad / Framework-class laptops are usually fine. Brand-new laptops, hybrid-GPU laptops, and peripherals that depend on Windows vendor software are where things break.

Most named

Nvidia GPUs

The single most-named hardware villain in older and many recent accounts: kernel updates breaking drivers, repeated fixing, closed drivers described as “delayed and generally just broken” (MakeUseOf). Improving with open kernel modules from RTX 20/40-series onward. Counterpoint: Pop!_OS Nvidia edition / distro-packaged drivers make this a non-event for many 2024–25 users — hardware- and distro-dependent, not universal.

Laptop-specific

Sleep, suspend & battery

s2idle / Modern Standby-only laptops draining in a bag (“hot bag”), battery shorter than Windows because power tuning and vendor optimisations are Windows-side, sleep draining overnight (XDA pile-up list). One of the most consistent laptop complaints in 2024–25 accounts.

Common

Hybrid graphics (iGPU + dGPU)

No automatic GPU switching, high idle temps, worse battery (UpThrust, 2020); still cited in 2025–26 guides: PRIME offload incantations, HDMI ports wired to the dGPU, black screen after driver install / Secure Boot MOK enrolment.

Chipset-dependent

Wi-Fi & Bluetooth

Wi-Fi is chipset-dependent — Broadcom and some newer MediaTek/Realtek historically worst; most Intel Wi-Fi is fine out of the box. The catch-22 (no driver, fix needs internet) recurs. Bluetooth: inconsistent audio reconnect / pairing in the XDA pile-up list; LTT 2026 cited Xbox controller pairing friction.

Often absent

Fingerprint & peripheral software

Fingerprint readers frequently simply unsupported — some ThinkPads work via libfprint, many don't. Razer Synapse, Logitech G Hub, Corsair iCUE, fan-curve / RGB / battery utilities: hardware works at a basic level, full features need the Windows app or community replacements (OpenRazer, Piper, Solaar). MakeUseOf

Improving

Displays, docks, printers, webcams

Wayland (GNOME/KDE) now handles per-monitor and fractional scaling much better, but XWayland apps still blur and MakeUseOf (2026) notes they “don't do well with fractional scaling,” plus mixed-refresh inconsistency. Thunderbolt docks mostly work in recent accounts. CUPS often works well (sometimes better than Windows for old printers); multifunction scanner/vendor software still lags. Webcams/mics usually work (UVC); vendor noise-cancellation / effects software does not.

5

Everyday friction: the system itself

None of these alone ends a trial. Together they are the “cost is your time” complaint — and for a long-time user in 2026, the choice burden alone was enough.

Choice overload comes first

Before anything breaks, a newcomer must choose: distro, desktop (GNOME vs KDE vs Cinnamon — “materially different” daily), update philosophy (rolling vs stable — which determines “when a Wi-Fi chip starts working or when a GPU regression is resolved”), packaging (Snap vs Flatpak vs native — none “universally right”), and display server (X11 vs Wayland, mid-transition). MakeUseOf's Mar 2026 piece is a decade-long Linux user quitting over exactly this fragmentation — evidence attrition isn't only beginners. XDA adds “choosing the wrong distro / wrong desktop” (e.g. starting on Arch and drowning) as a top beginner-quitting cause. MakeUseOf, Mar 2026

When it breaks, the fix is a terminal command

Modern Mint / Ubuntu / KDE users can do most daily tasks in a GUI. The complaint is specific: when something goes wrong, forum answers arrive as terminal commands. The Verge stayer: the terminal is “an unavoidable part of the Linux experience, even with… Snap Store and Flathub.” LTT 2021 is the canonical demonstration (see Stories).

Updates breaking things — and the fear of it

Kernel updates breaking Nvidia / out-of-tree drivers (XDA); rolling-release (Arch-family) breakage cited by multiple returners. Conversely, LTS / Mint users report boring stability. Distro-dependent — but the fear is widely cited even where the breakage isn't.

Streaming, HDR, files & fonts

No native Netflix / Prime / Disney+ apps; browser playback capped by Widevine L3 on Linux. Sources disagree on the exact cap — reported here as 720p–1080p depending on service/browser, no 4K/HDR — the consistent point is it's worse than Windows/Edge or a native app. HDR is Wayland-only with inconsistent washed-out/gamma issues; HDMI 2.1 VRR is broken on AMD Linux (HDMI Forum licensing). Plus .docx/.xlsx rendering differences, missing Microsoft fonts (Calibri etc.) changing pagination, and Excel macros/VBA not working in LibreOffice. Daily.dev HDR/Widevine summary · MakeUseOf

“I was too tired of fiddling with settings to even enjoy a game.”

— XDA, “I regret switching to Linux, even though I wanted to love it” (2026) — the same piece that frames the cost as time: “Linux is only ‘free’ to an extent.” Read at XDA

Support burden: there is no vendor to call — support is forums / Discord / Reddit. Experiences split sharply: stayers praise community help; returners cite “RTFM” / hostile replies and contradictory advice (“you chose the wrong distro” in response to any problem — noted even by XDA's sympathetic gaming returner). LTT 2026 cited “inconsistent community responses.” Workflow differences add to it: in LTT Pt.3's timed simple-tasks test (2021), digitally signing PDFs, adding fonts, and compressing/uploading files proved unexpectedly hard — some genuine missing polish, some unfamiliarity, as LTT's own community critics pointed out.

6

Work & school lock-in: often absolute

This blocker is not the user's choice, and it's the least documented in public posts — people don't blog about IT policy.

Explicit ban

Proctoring & exam software

Pearson VUE OnVUE (used by NICET and many cert bodies): “All Linux/Unix Operating Systems are strictly prohibited.” Examity: “does not support Linux or Chrome OS.” ProctorU / Guardian Browser lists Linux as unsupported. Respondus LockDown Browser and similar school tools are likewise Windows/Mac-only (widely reported; not separately verified in the research). The irony is documented by the Linux Professional Institute itself: OnVUE supports only Windows/macOS, so Linux-certification candidates must find a Windows/Mac machine or test in person; LPI has lobbied Pearson since OnVUE launched.

NICET / OnVUE requirements · Queen's — Examity · LPI on OnVUE

Narrow support

MDM, compliance & employer tools

Microsoft Intune does enrol Linux — but only Ubuntu Desktop 22.04/24.04 LTS and RHEL 8/9, GNOME, x86_64, corporate-owned devices only; personal-device enrolment is not supported. Any other distro, or Conditional Access requiring full Intune compliance / endpoint agents (CrowdStrike, GlobalProtect, Zscaler, Cisco Secure Client — Linux clients exist for some, with reduced features / parity lags), can lock a Linux user out of email, VPN and internal apps. In practice the most common form is simplest: the employer issues a Windows/Mac laptop and forbids alternatives.

Microsoft Learn — Enrol a Linux device in Intune

Not blockers by themselves: Teams and Zoom do work on Linux (native/web), and mainstream VPNs (WireGuard/OpenVPN native; major commercial clients) generally work. What pulls office workers back is the combination — Teams feature lag and Wayland screen-share quirks, mandated Office desktop for shared documents, and proprietary corporate VPN / zero-trust agents. Evidence here is moderate, mostly forum-level.

Who gives up, who stays

Outcome tracks the job, the game library and the hardware far more than enthusiasm. Filter the table — the pattern is the point.

User typeTypical outcomeWhy

Creator in the Adobe ecosystem / client workGives up / dual-bootsPremiere / Photoshop files, plugins, collaborators; alternatives don't read the workflow

Developer / sysadmin / data (Python etc.)Stays — highest success rateToolchain is native / better on Linux; the terminal is a plus, not friction

Single-player / indie gamerStaysProton covers the library; may accept losing 1–2 titles
Privacy- / tinker-motivated switcher (incl. PewDiePie-type)StaysMotivated by leaving Windows/Google; willing to pay the time cost; customisation is the reward

What stayers say they accepted

  • Replaced, not replicated, apps: LibreOffice for Office, RawTherapee for Lightroom, GIMP/Krita, DaVinci/Kdenlive — and accepted relearning
  • Accepted losing anti-cheat titles: “I don't play these types of games, so that didn't matter to me” — The Verge
  • Chose boring, stable setups (Mint, Fedora, Ubuntu LTS, Bazzite for gaming) and Linux-friendly hardware (AMD GPU, ThinkPad / Framework) rather than fighting hostile hardware
  • Accepted self-support as the model: “dozens of hours combing through Reddit threads, analyzing old Stack Overflow solutions” in a year (The Verge); learning fstab, Flatpak permissions, Proton launch options
  • Often kept an escape hatch: dual-boot, a second Windows/Mac machine, or a work-issued laptop — “pure” single-OS switchers are the minority even among success stories

“Linux isn't especially complicated on a daily basis, but you have to be willing to solve your own problems.”

— The Verge, year-long switcher · I spent a year on Linux and forgot to miss Windows

9

What got better, what didn't move

The net trajectory in the sources: the floor rose dramatically — the ceiling blockers did not, because they are publisher and vendor business decisions, not Linux engineering gaps.

✓ Materially improved by 2024–25

  • “Games don't run on Linux” (in general): Proton / Steam Deck (2022 onward) flipped the default — most single-player libraries now work. The LTT 2021 → 2026 contrast is the emblem
  • Installing Nvidia / basic drivers: distro-packaged drivers and gaming images (Pop!_OS, Bazzite, Nobara, CachyOS) mean the 2015-era compile-your-driver story is now a minority, new-hardware experience
  • Wayland basics: fractional scaling, multi-monitor mixed DPI, screen sharing via PipeWire portals, Nvidia-on-Wayland (driver 555+) — substantially matured 2023–25. Remaining complaints are edge cases, not “Wayland is unusable”
  • Everyday app availability: Flatpak / Flathub made Slack / Discord / Spotify / Zoom one-click; browsers, OBS, Blender, DaVinci, VS Code native. The old “can't even install normal apps / MP3 codecs” class has largely vanished
  • Wi-Fi / printers out of the box on mainstream (especially Intel-based) hardware: improved, fewer reports

✕ Persisting — essentially unchanged

  • Adobe Creative Cloud and Office desktop: still no native Linux versions, no announced plans. The #1 blocker in 2020 is the #1 blocker in 2025
  • Kernel anti-cheat: if anything regressed — Apex withdrew (Oct 2024), GTA Online added BattlEye (Sep 2024), EA moved to kernel Javelin. The EAC/BattlEye opt-in exists; top publishers don't opt in
  • Proctoring / MDM lock-in: OnVUE still Windows/Mac only despite years of LPI lobbying; Intune Linux support exists but is narrow (Ubuntu/RHEL only)
  • Laptop power / sleep: s2idle drain and shorter battery vs Windows still routinely reported; no structural fix because tuning is vendor / Windows-centric
  • Peripheral vendor software, fingerprint readers, CAD/DAW niches (Fusion, Ableton, iLok/plugins), streaming DRM caps (no 4K/HDR)
  • The meta-complaint: “free as in time” — self-support burden and fragmentation choice, present in LTT 2021, present in 2026 quitters

A web + Steam + dev user in 2025 has a genuinely good experience. The people who quit are mostly hitting the ceiling — and the ceiling is held up by decisions made in Adobe, Microsoft, Riot, EA and Pearson boardrooms, not in kernel changelogs.

— Synthesis of the trajectory across sources in the research (paraphrase of its net assessment, not a direct quote)
10

What this evidence can't tell you

Read the ranking as recurrence, not incidence. The research is explicit about its skews — they matter for how far to push any conclusion.

  • The public record is skewed. First-person accounts over-represent (a) tech-press listicles — XDA / MakeUseOf / How-To Geek publish many near-identical “dealbreakers” pieces, and several cited are prospective (“keeps me from switching,” notably MakeUseOf May 2026) rather than reports of an actual trial; (b) enthusiasts who succeeded; and (c) gamers/creators. Ordinary office workers who quietly fail rarely publish.
  • No reliable survey quantifying blocker frequency was found. The ranking on this page is by how often and how strongly items recur across sources — not measured incidence in the population of switchers.
  • Dates: several sources are dated 2026; they were used to check whether 2024–25 complaints persist, and are flagged where used (notably the LTT sequel). A few search-index dates/attributions (e.g. a “Proton 11 / Steam 3.95% Oct 2026” tracker article) could not be independently corroborated and were not relied on for core claims.
  • Streaming cap discrepancy: 720p (Widevine L3 summary) vs 1080p (MakeUseOf) — reported on this page as a range.
  • Reddit: direct Reddit threads were not opened in the research; forum-pattern claims rest on Lemmy / Autodesk / 4chan mirrors and secondary citations — thinner than the video/article evidence.
  • Open questions the research leaves open: how often each blocker is decisive in the general population (vs among people who publish); controlled measurements of Office file fidelity; and current, representative rates for docking and printer/scanner failures, where recent evidence is thin either way.
