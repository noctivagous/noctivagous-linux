# DIY / Self-Hosting Audience for a New Linux Desktop Distribution
## As represented by Hackaday and XDA Developers communities, 2024–2026

## Executive Summary

This is not a niche of people who want a prettier desktop. It is a large, maturing, production-like home-infrastructure audience:

- **Scale and seriousness:** selfh.st's annual survey grew from **4,081 completed responses in 2025** (verified live: https://selfh.st/survey/2025-results/) to a reported **6,221 in 2026** (index, secondary analysis: https://ettayeb.fr/en/selfhosted/selfhst-2026-survey-results-en/). The 2026 analysis reports 84% self-host sensitive data, 66% deploy a NAS, 62% run their own firewall and 86% use a custom domain — “less and less like a hobby and more and more like a small production environment.”
- **Motivations are control, not just thrift:** In an independent 850+ respondent 2025 survey, primary motivation was Fun/Hobby 31.3%, Privacy 16.7%, Independence from third parties 16.6%, Data control 12.5% (https://selfhosted-survey-2025.deployn.de/). Linuxiac's reporting of selfh.st 2025 likewise says “aside from the hobby aspect, most respondents said privacy was their main reason” and 81% run Linux (https://linuxiac.com/self-hosters-confirm-it-again-linux-dominates-the-homelab-os-space/).
- **The stack has converged:** Docker (near 9/10 in selfh.st 2025; 5,454 responses in 2026), Docker Compose files, Proxmox as hypervisor substrate (45% in 2025), Debian/Ubuntu as base, and a short repeatable app canon: **Jellyfin, Immich, Home Assistant, Nextcloud, Vaultwarden, Pi-hole/AdGuard Home, Paperless-ngx, Syncthing, Uptime Kuma, Forgejo/Gitea, *arr apps, Ollama/Open WebUI**.
- **The pain is operational, not installational:** XDA's year-in review — the single best primary source for §3 — names it plainly: non-automatic, sometimes-breaking updates; remote access as “its own project” (CGNAT, TLS, DNS); storage silently filling with logs/snapshots/caches; projects disappearing; and becoming unpaid IT for family who now depend on your server (verified live: https://www.Xda-developers.Com/i-self-hosted-foss-tools-for-a-year-and-these-are-the-operational-headaches-no-one-warns-you-about/).
- **Lock-in is the unforgivable sin, proven twice in 2025:** Plex paywalled remote playback of your own files (April 2025) and tripled its lifetime pass, driving Jellyfin migration; Synology restricted 2025 Plus NAS to its own branded drives and reversed it in DSM 7.3 (8 Oct 2025) after sales fell. Samsung removing OEM unlocking in One UI 8 is the Android equivalent. Any distro that adds a forced account, paywalled remote access, a hardware whitelist, or a non-open app-store gate will be read by this audience as the same move.
- **Opportunity for a desktop distro:** No mainstream desktop does the thing these users cobble together from Proxmox + Cockpit + CasaOS/YunoHost + Tailscale + Homepage/Uptime Kuma: a **desktop that is also a first-class, inspectable home server** — services centre, one-click-but-Compose-transparent installs, private-by-default remote access, explicit consent before anything is internet-facing, pre-update snapshots with one-click rollback, tested backups, and power/headless modes for always-on use. That gap, not another launcher, is the product.

**Confidence:** High for the stack, motivations and precedents (multiple surveys + project/community sources agree). Medium for exact 2026 percentages (selfh.st 2026 results page itself is chart-images; percentages here come from secondary analysis of it). XDA/Hackaday are editorial/project communities, not random samples — they show what this audience *does and celebrates*, not population prevalence.

## 1. Who They Are and What They Actually Do

### 1.1 Sub-groups — overlapping, not silos

| Sub-group | What they do | Dominant motivation | Signature evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Homelabbers** | Run Proxmox clusters, VMs/LXC, firewalls, NAS, test networks; often learn skills used at work | Learning, fun, control | selfh.st: Proxmox 45% (2025); clusters are a minority fringe in 2026 (736 of 6,221) vs consumer hardware 3,841 — most are one discreet/repurposed box |
| **Privacy/cost/control self-hosters** | Replace Google Photos/Drive, Netflix/Spotify, 1Password, Google Home with own services for themselves + family | Privacy, data control, independence, subscription fatigue | Motivations above; Docker's own survey summary lists privacy/data sovereignty, escaping subscription fatigue, avoiding lock-in, joy of customization (https://www.docker.com/blog/self-hosted-alternatives-control-your-data/?ref=dailydev) |
| **Android modders (XDA core)** | Unlock bootloader, root (Magisk/KernelSU), flash LineageOS/GrapheneOS/crDroid/Evolution X, microG, de-Google, keep old phones alive past OEM support | Control of own hardware, privacy, longevity, customisation | XDA forums are organised per-device into ROMs/Kernels/Recoveries; e.g. LineageOS builds for OnePlus One (bacon) still updated June 2025 with de-Googling tweaks — microG, non-Google captive-portal/SUPL/NTP (https://forum.xda-developers.com/t/rom-q-unofficial-optimized-lineageos-18-1-jan-6th-lineageos-17-1-jan-8th.3693223/) |
| **Hardware hackers / makers (Hackaday core)** | ESP32/Arduino/RP2040, 3D printing, soldering, salvaged panels/thin clients, custom PCBs, cyberdecks | Building itself; repurposing; learning | Hackaday front page pattern: Dell Wyse thin client → OpenWrt router after defeating a BIOS lock; salvaged laptop panels → monitors; a VTech toy → Arch Linux “PinkPad” laptop (https://hackaday.com/blog/?s=thin+client , https://hackaday.com/category/laptops-hacks/) |

Most real people are 2–3 of these at once: the XDA writer whose $150 mini-PC runs 12 containers runs Home Assistant + Immich + Pi-hole + Navidrome + Plex/Jellyfin (https://www.xda-developers.com/my-150-mini-pc-runs-12-docker-containers-and-hasnt-broken-a-sweat/); the Hackaday.io ControlPi builder racks many SBCs for “NAS, firewall and DNS services, and experimenting with lightweight, locally hosted language models,” managed via Docker + Portainer CE on a Pi Compute Module 5 touchscreen (https://hackaday.io/project/207185-controlpi).

### 1.2 What they build, modify and self-host

- **Home servers / homelabs:** Repurposed desktop, old laptop, used mini-PC (Dell OptiPlex Micro / Lenovo ThinkCentre Tiny / HP ProDesk Mini), Intel NUC, NAS, or Pi. XDA in 2026 explicitly argues old devices now beat a new Pi on price/performance after Pi price hikes, except on power (https://www.xda-developers.com/old-devices-make-better-home-servers-than-a-raspberry-pi/). An old laptop adds a built-in “UPS” (battery), screen/keyboard for rescue, and upgradable RAM/storage (https://www.xda-developers.com/my-old-laptop-makes-a-better-home-server-and-has-a-built-in-ups/).
- **Single-board computers:** Raspberry Pi remains the default on-ramp and top SBC (94% among SBC users in the independent 2025 survey), for Pi-hole, Home Assistant, lightweight/single-purpose servers. Limits are now openly discussed: SD-card wear, CPU/IO ceiling when stacking services, rising kit price. Hackaday.io's Solar Pi shows the extreme low-power end: Pi Zero W, Alpine in diskless (RAM) mode, lighttpd + file sharing, 1–2 W idle, months unattended (https://hackaday.io/project/205403-solar-pi/details).
- **NAS:** ~2/3 of selfh.st respondents deploy one; custom-built is the top NAS type (50%) in the independent survey — ahead of Synology/QNAP appliances. XDA's old-PC-to-NAS guide is representative: old gaming rig, IronWolf drives, TrueNAS Community Edition, with Unraid/OpenMediaVault as alternatives, and a warning that an old desktop pulls 40–110 W (https://www.xda-developers.com/how-turn-old-pc-into-nas/).
- **Media servers:** Jellyfin (now clearly ahead of Plex — see §3/§4), Plex for legacy/polish, Navidrome/Audiobookshelf, *arr automation (Sonarr/Radarr/Prowlarr).
- **Home automation:** Home Assistant dominates (29% run Home Assistant OS in selfh.st 2025). Hackaday shows the long tail beyond it: openHAB, MQTT devices, Zigbee (RaspBee/Dirigera), ESP8266/ESP32 endpoints, voice assistants built on Pi + Home Assistant specifically to leave Google/Amazon closed ecosystems (https://hackaday.com/tag/openhab/ , https://hackaday.com/tag/respeaker/).
- **Self-hosted cloud alternatives:** Nextcloud (files/calendar/contacts/office), Immich (photos), Vaultwarden (passwords), Paperless-ngx (documents), Syncthing (sync), plus bookmarks/notes/RSS/budgeting — i.e. one-for-one SaaS replacements. See §2.
- **Android ROMs / de-Googled phones / rooting:** Still active but *shrinking under vendor pressure* (§3). Workflows are unchanged for a decade: enable Developer Options (tap build number 7×) → OEM unlocking + USB debugging → `adb reboot bootloader` → `fastboot flashing unlock` (wipes device) → custom recovery (TWRP/Lineage Recovery) → ROM + optionally microG/F-Droid instead of Google apps. A desktop that makes ADB/fastboot painful (drivers, udev, outdated platform-tools) directly breaks this group's core workflow.
- **Repurposing old hardware:** Thin clients ($10–15 on eBay) as Pi alternatives running Debian/Proxmox/Home Assistant; old phones/tablets kept alive by LineageOS; old laptops as servers. Motivation is mixed: cost, e-waste aversion, and the Hackaday ethos that unused hardware “calls to be used.” Note the friction they celebrate overcoming — locked BIOS, oddball CPUs, integrated storage — is friction a distro could simply remove (§5).
- **Electronics / microcontroller projects:** Arduino, ESP32/ESP8266, RP2040/RP2350, Seeed XIAO, custom Linux-on-MCU builds, flashing via USB-serial, esptool, Arduino IDE/PlatformIO. Needs are mundane and specific: serial permission without sudo, stable device names, no ModemManager/brltty stealing the adapter, preinstalled cross-toolchains (§3, §5).

### 1.3 Who they are *not*
Not enterprise sysadmins: selfh.st 2025 discussion notes nearly all respondents self-host for personal use (only ~8 of 4,000+ indicated enterprise). Not non-technical consumers either: they read release notes, edit YAML, and accept being “sysadmin, network engineer, and on-call all at once” — but increasingly resent it, which is the design opening.

### 2.2 Tooling / orchestration norms

- **Docker is the substrate.** ~90% in selfh.st 2025; in 2026 Docker 5,454 responses vs LXC 1,863, Podman 790. Kubernetes at home is a minority taste: 9.6% in the independent survey; k3s appears in enthusiast builds (e.g. Pi 5 + k3s + Argo CD GitOps) but is not the norm. **Docker Compose is the unit of sharing:** guides, READMEs and “cookbooks” (150+ Compose recipes) all assume one folder + one `compose.yaml` per app, `docker compose up -d`, volumes for data, `.env` for secrets.
- **Proxmox VE is the substrate-under-the-substrate** (45%, selfh.st 2025): Debian-based, KVM + LXC in a web UI at :8006, typically running one VM/LXC for Docker, one for Home Assistant OS, one for TrueNAS. It does *not* do Docker or file shares natively on the host — users accept a VM layer to get isolation/snapshots.
- **NAS OSes:** TrueNAS (SCALE → renamed Community Edition in 25.04, Apr 2025), Unraid, OpenMediaVault — see §4.
- **One-click layers:** CasaOS, UmbrelOS, YunoHost, Runtipi, Coolify (PaaS-style, popular in Pi homelab guides), Cosmos, StartOS — see §4. Their existence proves demand for a GUI; their fragmentation proves no one has nailed it on a general-purpose distro.
- **Container UIs are churning:** Portainer fell from 66% to 33% share among panel users in one year (selfh.st 2026, after Portainer announced the end of Community Edition feature development in favour of a Kubernetes-centred 3.0 capped free tier) — users fled to Dockge, Dockhand, Komodo, Arcane. Lesson: this audience punishes rug-pulls instantly and migrates in months.
- **Databases:** 82% (2026) run a separate containerised DB per app rather than a shared instance — learned the hard way that shared DBs make upgrades brittle.
- **Base OS:** Debian (2,954) and Ubuntu (2,584) dominate in 2026, well ahead of Arch/Fedora/Alpine. Stability + every tutorial assuming Debian is self-reinforcing.

### 2.3 Installation / update / backup patterns they *expect*

1. **Install:** official Docker image + official Compose file, or one-click from a catalogue that *shows* that Compose file. Data in a named, visible directory/volume they can copy. No curl-pipe-to-bash as the only path (though many guides still do it for Coolify etc.).
2. **Update:** `pull + up -d`, pinned or at least visible image tags (not blind `latest` for stateful apps), release notes *before* updating, and a backup/snapshot taken first. Watchtower-style blind auto-update is controversial for stateful services (see Immich, §3).
3. **Backup:** 3-2-1, with Borg/Restic as the respected engines; app-level DB dump + files, in that order, with a copy *off the box*. Zerobyte (Restic + web UI) being spotlighted by selfh.st in Nov 2025 is a signal of unmet demand for backup UX (https://selfh.st/weekly/2025-11-21/).
4. **Rebuildability is the new uptime:** mature users keep Compose/Ansible in Git, secrets outside the repo, and judge a setup by “can I rebuild it from scratch on a fresh Debian install?”

## 3. Frictions and Pain Points

### 3.1 Self-hosting itself

- **Updates break things — the #1 documented complaint.** XDA (verified live, above): “Self-hosted projects ship changes on their own schedule, so the chances that something breaks when you update is really high… Immich recently moved the server's internal port from 3001 to 2283, which would break your setup unless you made edits to your Compose file and the reverse proxy.” Immich's own releases repeat the pattern at scale: v1.133.0 required a DB vector-extension migration (pgvecto.rs → VectorChord), a DB backup first, matching mobile/server versions, and warned “you should not downgrade below 1.133.0” (https://github.com/immich-app/immich/releases/tag/v1.133.0). TrueNAS rewrote its entire apps stack (k3s → Docker Compose in 24.10) with a hard 1 June 2025 auto-migration cutoff (https://github.com/laoujin/atlas/blob/HEAD/research/2026-04-28-replacing-a-synology-nas-in-2026-hardware-os-migration-and-remote-access/os-and-storage-stack-without-lock-in/index.md).
- **Remote access is a second project.** Choosing between port-forward + reverse proxy, overlay VPN, VPS relay, or tunnel — “none of those are obviously correct for a beginner, and it compounds if you're behind CGNAT… you've now taken on the burden of TLS certs, DNS records, and other configurations that accumulate” (XDA, above).
  - **CGNAT:** your router's port-forward rule does nothing when the ISP shares one public IPv4 among customers (WAN in 100.64.0.0/10); detection alone requires comparing router WAN vs public IP (https://www.ssdnodes.com/learn/how-to-self-host-a-website).
  - **Dynamic IP / DDNS, ISP-blocked 80/443, double NAT** are each separate failure modes in the same chain: DNS → router → firewall → proxy → app; if one link fails, everything fails (https://lucasbittencourt.dev/blog/self-hosting-at-home).
  - **TLS:** Caddy automates Let's Encrypt; nginx+certbot and Traefik label-config fail silently (renewal failure “breaks TLS silently until certificate expires”; a missing Caddy `/data` volume loses ACME keys and hits Let's Encrypt rate limits for up to 7 days) (https://umbrel.sbs/en/posts/caddy-reverse-proxy-in-docker-auto-https-and-clean-config/).
  - **The workaround became a dependency:** Tailscale (WireGuard + hosted control plane + DERP relays) and Cloudflare Tunnel solve CGNAT by outbound connection, which is why they won — but self-hosters then worry about the hosted control plane, default connection logging, and outages (selfh.st noted Cloudflare/GitHub/Tailscale outages “impacting self-hosted infrastructure across the globe” in one week, Nov 2025). Headscale/NetBird/Pangolin exist purely to self-host that control plane (https://homelabber.info/guides/tailscale-vs-wireguard , https://www.projecthomelab.org/security/remote-access/).
- **Backup/restore is where data is actually lost.** Immich's automatic DB dumps write to the *same disk* as the photos and contain *no photos* — “a dead drive takes out your photos and the backup of their metadata in one go” (https://siops.app/blogs/how-to-self-host-immich-without-losing-a-single-photo). USENIX's study of 994 self-hosters + 41 interviews found security approached “in an unstructured way,” with “vague catch-all concepts such as firewalls and backups” dominating without reflection on the threat mitigated (https://www.usenix.org/user?destination=conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/grober). Restores are rarely tested until they fail.
- **Monitoring/alerting:** services fail *silently* — Immich's web UI loads while its ML container OOM-crashes repeatedly; phone uploads stop and “the server never noticed anything was missing.” Hence Uptime Kuma's ubiquity and the mature-lab pattern of an alert *when a backup doesn't run*, not just when a service is down.
- **Storage fills invisibly:** “Logs are the classic killer, but snapshots, local backups, preview files, caches, indexes… can pile up fast” (XDA). No mainstream desktop warns a hoster that a disk is 90% full of Docker overlays and thumbnails.
- **Power, noise, heat:** an old desktop NAS at 40–110 W idle, a rack server ~150 W and loud, vs a Pi at 1–5 W and a mini-PC/NUC as the accepted compromise (~quiet, low power, enough CPU). XDA's whole old-device genre is this trade-off. Always-on also means power cuts: no UPS = unreachable password vault/photos exactly when needed.
- **Security exposure & responsibility:** the community norm flipped from “forward 80/443 to Traefik” to “open no ports; VPN/tunnel + SSO in front.” Exposing admin UIs (Portainer, Cockpit :9090, Proxmox :8006) to the internet is explicitly warned against everywhere. And once family depend on your Jellyfin/Immich/Vaultwarden, downtime is not deferrable — “you're now responsible for getting things back online” (XDA).
- **Projects disappear / get acquired / change terms:** Booklore (10k+ stars) vanished overnight — repo, Discord, site — users discovered it via a 404 on `docker pull`; it survived only because it could be forked (Grimmory) (XDA). Portainer CE, Plex, Synology drives, Gitea→Forgejo flight, and Arduino/Qualcomm (below) are the same pattern.

### 3.2 Mainstream Linux desktops / distros

- **No server mode:** desktop distros assume a person at a screen who sleeps/suspends the machine, uses NetworkManager Wi-Fi, and reboots for updates. Homelab use needs the opposite defaults: no suspend, lid-close = keep serving (old-laptop servers), wired/static IP, wake-on-LAN, services surviving logout, headless rescue via SSH/web. Users currently bolt this on with `systemctl mask sleep.target`, logind.conf edits and luck.
- **Permissions & file sharing:** Samba sharing from GNOME/KDE still routinely requires editing `smb.conf`, installing plugins, or fixing fstab/CIFS `permission denied` errors; containers add a second permission universe (PUID/PGID, rootful Docker socket, bind-mount ownership) that no desktop UI explains. A service that can't read the media folder is the most common first-week failure.
- **Hardware-hacking friction (dialout/udev hell):** Arduino/ESP32/3D-printer/SDR/ADB all present as `/dev/ttyUSB0`/`ttyACM0` owned by `root:dialout` (Debian) or `root:uucp` (Arch) — the group name differs by distro, adding yourself requires re-login, and forum answers still include `chmod 666` and hand-written udev rules with single-vs-double `=` typos that silently fail (https://bbs.archlinux.org/viewtopic.php?pid=1594592 , https://umatechnology.org/linux-usb-not-detected-or-not-working-5-common-issues-and-fixes/). Flatpak sandboxing compounds it: a sandboxed IDE/flashing tool cannot see the serial device at all.
- **Telemetry, forced accounts, walled gardens — read as betrayal, not inconvenience:**
  - **Plex:** free remote playback removed Apr 2025; lifetime pass $119.99 → $249.99 → $749.99 (July 2026) (https://www.howtogeek.com/plex-keeps-getting-more-expensive-heres-what-people-are-switching-to/).
  - **Samsung/Android:** One UI 8 (July 2025) removed the OEM-unlocking toggle *in every region* — XDA contributor analysis found `androidboot.other.locked=1` now active globally; without unlock there is “no custom recovery, no ROM, no root” (https://thecustomrom.com/news/samsung-galaxy-s25-custom-rom-progress). Xiaomi/OnePlus added server-side permission, waiting periods and quotas; Google's Play Integrity API lets banking apps refuse rooted/ROM devices. Beebom's 2025 ROM roundup notes custom-ROM popularity “has dropped significantly” as OEM update promises lengthened *and* Play Integrity tightened (https://beebom.com/best-custom-roms-android-phones/).
  - **Arduino/Qualcomm (Nov 2025):** new terms perceived as granting Arduino rights over user creations, banning reverse engineering and expanding data collection triggered immediate backlash (amplified by Adafruit); Arduino had to publish “anything that was open, stays open,” clarifying the restrictions were meant only for its Cloud SaaS (https://www.hackster.io/news/arduino-clarifies-terms-and-conditions-following-backlash-anything-that-was-open-stays-open-645e9ee9a51e). The lesson is about *trust velocity*: this audience reads terms changes within hours.
  - **Synology:** §4.
- **Driver/firmware tinkering:** Jellyfin transcoding needs Intel Quick Sync with the render group set correctly; Wi-Fi/BT firmware, NVIDIA, and HAT/GPIO support on SBCs are distro-specific. Hackaday builds routinely stall on BIOS locks, non-booting CPUs and Pi-specific software assumptions when moving to generic x86.

## 4. How Existing Systems Accommodate Them — Precedents, Good and Bad

| System | Pattern that works | What drew criticism / limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Synology DSM / QNAP QTS** | Polished web desktop metaphor, Package Center, snapshots, QuickConnect relay (no port-forward), drive health in UI. The usability bar for NAS. | **2025 drive lock-in:** Plus models (DS925+ etc.) refused storage pools on non-Synology drives; health/dedup/firmware gated. Reversed in DSM 7.3 (8 Oct 2025) after sales “plummeted” and backlash; M.2 NVMe pools *still* require listed drives (https://consumerrights.wiki/w/Synology_requiring_proprietary-branded_drives_to_be_used_with_its_NAS). QNAP's history: ransomware exposure of internet-facing NAS. Lesson: polish wins users, lock-in loses them permanently. |
| **TrueNAS (SCALE → Community Edition)** | Best-in-class ZFS UI: pools, snapshots, replication, SMB/NFS, GPU passthrough for transcode. Free. | Opinionated/heavy (8 GB RAM floor, ZFS-only); **apps stack rewritten twice** (k3s → Docker Compose) breaking user apps; “Docker Apps can be finicky compared to raw Docker Compose” (https://selfhosting-sh.pages.dev/hardware/best-home-server-os/). |
| **Unraid** | Mixed-size drive array (each disk independently readable on any Linux box), huge Community Apps store (2,000+ Docker templates), VM + GPU passthrough, friendly WebGUI, onboarding wizard (7.3). | Proprietary, paid: Starter $49 / Unleashed $109 / Lifetime $249, + optional $36/yr for further feature updates (perpetual licence, security patches for supported minors) (https://kextcache.com/best-os-for-home-servers/). Proves users *will* pay once for polish — and argue endlessly about anything that smells like subscription. |
| **Proxmox VE** | Free (AGPL), Debian base, excellent web UI for VMs/LXC/ZFS/backup, clustering, snapshot/rollback. The homelab default. | No Docker, no file shares on host (by design); networking (bridges/VLANs) learning curve; subscription nag screen; enterprise repo upsell. Users run Docker *inside* a VM to work around it. |
| **CasaOS (IceWhale)** | Layer (not OS) on Debian/Ubuntu/Pi OS: friendly dashboard, file/drive widgets, one-click curated store (Nextcloud/Jellyfin/Vaultwarden/HA), custom Docker import for off-catalogue apps. Lowest-friction start. | No built-in reverse proxy/HTTPS, no SSO/auth gateway, **no built-in backup** — “you fill in security/reverse-proxy/backup yourself”; company focus shifted to commercial ZimaOS, CasaOS described as in maintenance mode as of Sept 2026 (https://www.bigiron.cc/guides/casaos-vs-yunohost-vs-cosmos , https://Proofconference.com/book/en/self-hosting/). |
| **UmbrelOS** | Most polished personal-server UX; curated store (~300 apps, strong Bitcoin/Lightning + media), one-click installs, 2FA. Started as Bitcoin node, became general. | **PolyForm Noncommercial “source-available” licence — not open source**, cannot be freely forked/redistributed; curated store = obscure apps added by hand; less fun “once you start changing defaults.” Polish + non-open licence is exactly the trade this audience debates. |
| **YunoHost** | The most *complete* open precedent: Debian, webadmin + user portal, **~500 packaged apps**, LDAP **SSO for all apps**, built-in email (Postfix/Dovecot + DKIM/SPF/DMARC guidance), one-click Let's Encrypt, multi-domain, **diagnosis tool** (DNS/ports/email/certs), built-in backup/restore. AGPL, non-profit/community. | Opinionated, single-server, package (not Docker) model, plainer UI, email deliverability still hard. But architecturally it is the closest existing answer to §5 — a desktop distro should study its diagnosis + SSO + backup integration hardest. |
| **Home Assistant OS** | Appliance model done right: minimal OS whose *only* job is HA + Supervisor + **Add-ons** (curated Docker with config UI, ingress, backups), OTA OS updates with rollback, restore-on-fresh-install flow. Non-HAOS installs (Core/DietPi) *lose add-ons* — a constant beginner trap in its forums (https://community.home-assistant.io/t/ha-problem-setting/884968). | Single-purpose; add-on store is HA-gated; not a general server. Lesson: users choose the appliance install *for* the managed add-on/backup/update system, not for HA itself. |
| **OpenMediaVault / DietPi / Raspberry Pi OS** | OMV: thin Debian veneer + web UI + full `apt` escape hatch, runs on 1 GB. DietPi: 30–40 MB RAM idle, `dietpi-software` curated pre-configured installs (Pi-hole, Nextcloud, HA, WireGuard), fully headless from first boot, thrives on Pi Zero/old boards. Pi OS: Lite/Desktop split, Imager with pre-seeded Wi-Fi/SSH/user — the best first-boot headless provisioning UX in the field. | OMV/DietPi UIs are functional, not polished; plugin/extra layers can lag the base. Pi OS is Pi-specific. Lesson: *lightweight + curated installer + escape hatch* beats a monolith on old hardware. |
| **FreedomBox / StartOS** | FreedomBox (Debian, web UI for privacy services) proved demand for appliance-on-Debian a decade ago. StartOS (MIT, Start9): sovereignty-first, encrypted backups + WireGuard built in, Tor optional, multiple registries + sideloading allowed. | Smaller catalogues, more to learn (StartOS); FreedomBox pace/audience stayed niche. Lesson: sovereignty features are differentiators, catalogue breadth is table stakes. |
| **Cockpit (Red Hat) — desktop/server bridge** | The most instructive *desktop-distro* precedent: web console at :9090, **socket-activated (zero cost when idle)**, **no own state/database** — it calls systemd/NetworkManager/udisks/Podman via the same APIs as the CLI, so GUI and terminal never disagree. Modules: services+inline journal, logs, storage (LVM/RAID/SMART), networking/firewall, updates, Podman containers, libvirt VMs, in-browser terminal. Log in with the *system* account (PAM) (https://linuxdork.com/blog/cockpit-web-console/). | Self-signed cert warning on first visit; must never be exposed to the internet; Podman-not-Docker focus mismatches homelab Docker norm; no app catalogue. Lesson: adopt its *architecture* (stateless view over real system APIs) even if you ship a native UI. |
| **Webmin / terminal-first distros** | Webmin: breadth (everything configurable in browser) for 25+ years. Terminal-first (plain Debian + Compose in Git) remains the power-user gold standard for rebuildability. | Webmin: dated UX, huge attack surface, own config layer that can diverge from the system. Terminal-only: excludes the household admin and the “second machine at home” use case. |
| **Android developer model (XDA lens)** | Developer Options hidden behind a deliberate gesture, OEM unlock toggle, `adb`/`fastboot` as official tools, unlockable Pixel as reference device, verified-boot *warning* rather than refusal, relockable bootloader on GrapheneOS with custom keys. | The model is being dismantled vendor-by-vendor (§3): greyed-out OEM unlock (carrier), server-side permission + countdowns (Xiaomi), toggle removal (Samsung), Play Integrity punishment after unlock. XDA's lesson for a desktop distro: **an unlock/developer mode that exists on day one and is removed by an update destroys trust more than never offering it.** |

**UI patterns that demonstrably work (synthesis):** (1) tile app catalogue with one-click install *and* visible underlying Compose/package; (2) a single dashboard that is both launchpad (Homepage-style) and health view (Uptime Kuma-style); (3) web-admin reachable at `hostname.local` / a stable LAN name, plus native desktop UI for the machine in front of you — users use both; (4) logs/metrics per service, in place, not in a separate observability stack; (5) one-click HTTPS + one-click *private* remote access (VPN) as distinct, labelled choices; (6) snapshot-before-update + one-click rollback (Proxmox/TrueNAS/HAOS); (7) community stores/recipes as *additional registries*, not a single gatekeeper (StartOS/Umbrel community stores, Unraid Community Apps, HACS for Home Assistant).

## 5. Design Implications for a Desktop-First Linux Distro

Separate **proven demand** (evidenced above) from **speculative** (plausible, no direct community evidence).

### 5.1 Proven demand — build these

1. **Two host modes, first-class in Settings:**
   - *Host on this machine:* turning the desktop into an always-on host flips a coherent profile — inhibit suspend/sleep, lid-close keeps serving, enable SSH + web admin, static/DHCP-reserved IP prompt, firewall profile for a server, update policy = notify-not-reboot.
   - *Host on another machine at home:* the desktop as *console* for a headless box (the old laptop/mini-PC/Pi in a cupboard): discover it, provision it (Pi Imager-style pre-seeding of user/Wi-Fi/SSH keys), manage its services remotely. This matches how people actually grow: desktop first, second box second.
2. **Services / Containers Centre (native + web twin):** Docker Compose as the native format (import any `compose.yaml` from a URL/Git/file; export everything back out — no lock-in). Catalogue entries are *recipes wrapping the upstream Compose*, showing image, ports, volumes, and a diff before update. Start/stop/restart, per-service logs, resource use, “open web UI,” edit `.env` with secrets masked. Stateless-over-the-system architecture à la Cockpit: the GUI edits the real Compose/systemd state, so terminal and GUI never diverge.
3. **Exposure permission model — the flagship interaction:** every service is **LAN-only by default**. Making anything internet-facing is an explicit, per-service ASK, modelled like a phone permission: *“Immich — Allow access from the internet? [Via my VPN only (recommended)] [Public, with HTTPS + login] [Cancel]”* — showing the port, the domain, whether auth/SSO is in front, and a plain-language risk line. Revocable in one place: a single “What's exposed?” screen listing every public port, tunnel and share. This directly answers the community's #1 safety failure (unstructured exposure) without paternalism.
4. **Remote access wizard that handles the ugly chain:** detect CGNAT (WAN vs public IP), dynamic IP, blocked ports; then offer, in order: Tailscale/WireGuard VPN (private, recommended), self-hosted control plane (Headscale/NetBird), tunnel (Cloudflare/Pangolin), or classic port-forward + DDNS + reverse proxy + Let's Encrypt via Caddy-style automation. One flow provisions DDNS, proxy route and certificate together, and *tests it from outside the LAN* before declaring success (YunoHost's diagnosis tool is the precedent).
5. **Update safety:** pre-update snapshot (btrfs/ZFS/timeshift-style for the OS; DB dump + volume snapshot for each service), release-notes/breaking-change flagging (parse upstream “BREAKING CHANGE / Action Required” — Immich-style), staged updates (update one service, health-check, proceed), and **one-click rollback including downgrade of the data migration where possible, with an honest warning where it isn't.** Never auto-update stateful services silently; auto-apply *security* updates to the OS with a scheduled window.
6. **Backup that is tested, not just configured:** guided 3-2-1 setup (local disk + off-box copy), Borg/Restic engine, per-service “what actually needs backing up” (DB + files, in order — not the vendor's misleading default), encryption, and **scheduled restore drills** with a green/red “last restore test” badge. Alert when a backup *doesn't* run.
7. **Health & alerting built in:** service-down, disk->85%, container OOM/restart-loop, certificate-expiring, backup-missed, and update-available notifications to the desktop *and* a phone-reachable channel (ntfy/Gotify/email). A Homepage/Uptime-Kuma-style dashboard as the default “Home” view for host mode, with widgets for other machines/services in the house.
8. **LAN discovery & sharing without the Samba ritual:** mDNS/Bonjour advertisement (`jellyfin.local`, `nas.local`), one-click folder share (SMB + a browser link) with user/password creation inline, NFS/SFTP toggles, and a “devices & services on my network” browser. Sharing must work from the file manager's right-click, with the firewall opened *for that share only* and shown in the exposure screen.
9. **Power & always-on controls:** idle watts estimate, per-service wake behaviour, scheduled sleep/wake, WoL send/receive, UPS (NUT) status widget, laptop-battery-as-UPS mode (warn + graceful service shutdown at low battery), fan/noise profile, and “turn the screen off but keep serving.” Old hardware is the target: the whole host stack must idle light (DietPi/Cockpit socket-activation as the bar: near-zero when nobody is looking).
10. **Hardware-hacking support out of the box:** a “Developer / Maker” settings page that (a) offers one-click serial access (adds user to the correct group — whatever this distro calls it — without re-login pain), (b) ships curated udev rules for Arduino/ESP32/ESP8266, STM32, RP2040, CH340/CP210x/FTDI adapters, 3D printers, SDR, and Android (`adb`/`fastboot` with correct permissions, current platform-tools), (c) preinstalled/optional esptool, Arduino IDE/PlatformIO, OpenOCD, `picocom` — all *unsandboxed or correctly device-passed-through* when installed as Flatpaks, (d) GPIO/I2C/SPI enablement on SBC builds, (e) a serial monitor and a “what is this USB device?” view (lsusb/udev properties in human form). Plus an Android companion view: detect a plugged phone, offer ADB pairing, scrcpy, file transfer, and flashing-tool status.
11. **Openness guarantees, stated as policy:** no forced account (account, if any, is for optional sync only and the server works fully without it); no telemetry by default (opt-in, with a local log of what would be sent); sideloading and custom catalogue registries/repos as a supported, documented path; every catalogue app removable with its data exportable; OS and catalogue licences shown per app (flag source-available like Umbrel's, don't present it as open source). Publish these as commitments — this audience *will* check after the first controversial update.
12. **Terminal & scripting openness:** first-class terminal, systemd unit viewer/editor in Settings (unit status, journal, enable/disable — Cockpit's services page natively), cron/timer UI, and everything the Services Centre does also available as a CLI/API so setups stay in Git and rebuildable.

### 5.2 Speculative / nice-to-have — validate before building
- Local-AI centre (Ollama/Open WebUI one-click, GPU detection) — demand is real in self-hosting generally (2026 growth), but whether users want it *from their desktop distro* vs a container is unproven.
- Household user management / family SSO (authentik-style) bundled — YunoHost proves it for multi-user servers; single-admin homes may not need it. Offer as an optional recipe, not core identity.
- Phone companion app for the distro's dashboard — Uptime Kuma/HA apps suggest appetite; building/maintaining mobile apps is a large commitment.
- One-click public *publishing* (blogs/sites/fediverse) — YunoHost territory; most homelab demand is private/family access first.
- Energy-cost (£/$ per month) estimation per service — plausible delight, no direct survey evidence.

### 5.3 Anti-recommendations (proven to backfire)
Forced account or cloud relay as the *only* remote path (Plex); paywalling remote access/transcoding; hardware/drive whitelists (Synology); removing a developer/unlock feature in an update (Samsung); a single curated store with no sideload/compose import (Umbrel/CasaOS limits); blind auto-updates of stateful services (Immich/TrueNAS breakage); a web admin that maintains its own divergent config database (Webmin anti-pattern vs Cockpit).

## Prioritized Product / UI Recommendations

**P0 — ship with v1 (the differentiator):**
1. Host Mode profile (this machine / another machine) with always-on, headless and power defaults (§5.1.1).
2. Services Centre: Compose-native, catalogue + import-your-own, logs/metrics/update-with-notes (§5.1.2).
3. Exposure ASK + “What's exposed?” screen; LAN-only default (§5.1.3).
4. Remote-access wizard: CGNAT detection → VPN-first → tunnel → port-forward/DDNS/HTTPS, with external test (§5.1.4).
5. Snapshot-before-update + rollback; backup with restore-test badge and missed-backup alerts (§5.1.5–6).

**P1 — fast follow:**
6. Health dashboard + phone-reachable alerts (§5.1.7). 7. One-click LAN sharing + network browser (§5.1.8). 8. Maker/Developer page: serial/udev/ADB/fastboot/esptool done right (§5.1.10). 9. Power/UPS/WoL controls (§5.1.9).

**P2 — validate first:** local-AI centre, household SSO, companion app (§5.2).

**Standing policy (costs nothing, worth most):** no forced account, no default telemetry, sideloading/custom registries supported, per-app licence shown, developer features never removed by update (§5.1.11).
